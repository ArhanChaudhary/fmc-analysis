The questions, in the askers' own words, and where each is answered. Most got an opinion or no answer at the time.

**Better switch guidelines (sections 4 to 7)**

- *find better guidelines than we have already (arm, drm, pairs, maybe rodney's corner orbit thing) for when to switch an eo, closer to 100% vs 0% chance of sub7 to dr. Guidelines that humans can quickly check.* *Answer*: count the side turns with U and D free: for 4c4e the half turns to two bars (section 4), for the other DRMs the side turns (section 5), with a certain class at one end and a near-certain discard at the other. For a pair trace alone, the switch cards of section 6. All read from the normal side as in section 7.

**Which feature says most (sections 6, 10 and 11)**

- JTuu ([[T4332]]): "it might be useful to have a table comparing P(optimal < N | rzp) against P(optimal < N | rzp, arm). that is, when is the arm info most informative?" eff ([[T8206]], [[T8207]]): "yeah is there an objective comparison between the two yet", "arm-xcxe vs xy-z with n pairs". *Answer*: pairs settle more cases than ARM for 4c4e, 4c2e and 7c8e, ARM more for 8c8e and 2c2e, and for 4c4e paired edges plus edge ARM settle more than pairs and ARM together (section 10). The information measures are in section 11 (**computer only**).
- wen ([[D14349]]): "Interestingly now we have basically 3 criteria for NISS that we can trace, >1pair/ARM/corner structure", "if we pick 2 out of 3 to stick to, which gives the lowest false negative rate?" *Answer*: pairs + corner case, by a little over pairs + ARM; ARM + corner case is far behind. Paired edges + edge ARM, which isn't on wen's list, settles more cases than any two of the three (section 10).
- Zagros ([[T11345]]): "Is number of corners NOT in a pair a better heuristic across all DRM than number of pairs?" *Answer*: once you know the DRM they say the same thing (section 10).
- tseitsei89 ([[T9334]]): "It would be really nice to find a way to filter out good 1 pair cases but there doesnt seem to be one currently". *Answer*: the 1-pair cases 2 half turns from two bars, {= n(RPH(1, '2', 'n')) =} of them at {= pcx(RPH(1, '2')) =} (three ring pictures, appendix A); by a pair trace alone, edge ARM, then the corner case (section 6.1).
- bpaul ([[D12986]], [[D12987]]): "random thought i wonder whether looking at pair stats not just on the count but also based on the number of pairs per edge could be good", "sounds way harder to count for higher corner counts but maybe for 4c4e it would be helpful idk". *Answer*: yes, and it's the main 4c4e finding (sections 6.1 and 10).
- caycala ([[D11050]]): "i wonder what the probability is for 21 + no pairs+ 1 corner pair". *Answer*, reading "corner pair" as a side pair: 4c4e ARM 21 with no pairs and 1 side pair is {= pc(Q4('4c4e ARM 21, 0 pairs, 1 side pair(s)')) =}; with 2 side pairs it is {= pc(Q4('4c4e ARM 21, 0 pairs, 2 side pair(s)')) =}.
- FakeMMAP ([[D12097]]): "Do you think it's worthwhile to check for ARM if you have 0 pairs on the off-chance it's a short setup to F2 B2 R?" *Answer*: with 0 pairs, ARM 00 is {= pc(Q4('4c4e, 0 pairs, ARM 00')) =} and ARM 42 is {= pc(Q4('4c4e, 0 pairs, ARM 42')) =}, but each is only 36 of the 21,000 zero-pair cases. Every other ARM is {= pc(Q4('4c4e, 0 pairs, ARM 21')) =} or less (ARM 21 is the best of them). So it's only worth it if the ARM check costs you nothing. The ring picture finds the case directly: `F2 B2 R` is the one 0-pair picture that is 2 half turns from two bars, in each layer two bad corners pointing at each other across a good edge place with the bad edge opposite (`oEo->-<-` twice, {= n(RPH(0, '2', 'n')) =} cases at {= pcx(RPH(0, '2')) =}).

The cases worth it are the short ones: an R or L turn that leaves a 4c4e one half turn from two bars ({= pcx(RSHORT('5c4e')) =}, {= pcx(RSHORT('5c6e')) =} and {= pcx(RSHORT('6c6e')) =} for 5c4e, 5c6e and 6c6e, on 3% to 4% of each; section 5.4).
