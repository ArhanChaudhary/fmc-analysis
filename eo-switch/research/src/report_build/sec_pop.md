## 6. Real RZPs: does the uniform table apply after a switch?

Every table on the server counts each case once. Real cases come from RZPs, and torchlight pointed out why that matters: "if the EO isn't already 4c4e, it means that the 4c4e case you get from RZP is 1 move away from something that is not 4c4e", "the 4c4e cases that can go out of 4c4e in more ways will thus be more likely to show up" ([[T4250]]). KrzychXyz asked whether natural 4c4e is therefore better ([[T4265]]). **On the same side the bias is real but small** (4c4e {= pc(POP('4c4e', 0)) =} against {= pc(POP('4c4e', 1)) =}). **After the switch it is gone exactly.** The inverse of a cube is uniform over the cubes with the same case (twist counts swapped, the same E-slice edges out), whatever path made the case. So the switched case is uniform, and an RZP's choice only picks the DRM. For every named DRM except 6cXe and 8cXe that leaves nothing to choose (one class, or two mirror classes with equal rates), and for those two the mix of twist splits (4+4 or 7+1 for 8c8e) could shift, which the test on real attempts below covers. So **the exhaustive tables, and the cards, are exactly right for switched RZPs.** The real attempts agree. In {= n(E['uniform_cells']) =} cells (12 DRMs × RZP to 4, 5 or 6), the attempts' rate is never more than {= f2(E['uniform_worst_z']) =} standard errors from the uniform rate of their own mix (errors from 20 batches of 300 scrambles). This also answers KrzychXyz: natural cases are slightly better on the same side and identical after a switch.

**RZP length.** The last column of the table is the real attempts with a 6-move RZP, where the target is DR in 5. The rates fall by a factor of {= f1(min(POP(d, 3) / POP(d, 4) for d in E['pop'])) =} to {= f1(max(POP(d, 3) / POP(d, 4) for d in E['pop'])) =}, which is why solvers use a shorter cut for 6-move RZPs. The cards give DR in 5 and DR in 7 for every cell (section 4).

**The last turns after a switch.** Turning the RZP's last move the other way never changes the switched case: in {= n(E['last_rzp'][0]) =} of {= n(E['last_rzp'][1]) =} pairs of real attempts that differ only in that, the switched case is the same. The reason: on the inverse that move becomes the first premove, and a premove from the DR group (R and R' differ by R2) doesn't change which pieces are twisted or which places hold E-slice edges relative to home. Jay's idea of looking at the case when you "flip" the RZP ([[T6694]]) is therefore no help after a switch.
