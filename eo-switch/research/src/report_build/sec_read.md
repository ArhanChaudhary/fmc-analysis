## 7. Reading the switched case from normal

You decide before switching, so every feature of the cards has to be seen on the normal side, after EO + RZP. I tested each reading below on uniformly random cubes with the EO solved (a random case, then random pieces): read it on the normal cube, compare with the feature of its inverse. The pair tracing is wen's, as the guide puts it: "look at one of the edges in the E slice that don't belong there, then look at a misoriented corner, if the 2 stickers on the corner that are touching the E slice match with that E slice edge, then that's a pair on inverse". For the 4c4e card, note which E-slice edge each matching corner belongs to: two different edges is *both paired*. The corner-ARM count is in rodneykinney's doc as "It's easy to predict how many corners will be out of AR on inverse: count the number of R/L stickers on the U/D face." Jay had it 14 months before the doc, with the edge count next to it (below).

**The two rings.** A piece that is out of place on normal marks its *own* place on the inverse. So the inverse's U ring is your U-coloured pieces and its D ring your D-coloured pieces, and you fill in the rings of section 4.1 by colour: with white on U and green on F, the U ring is white-blue, white-blue-red, white-red, white-red-green, white-green, white-green-orange, white-orange, white-orange-blue.

- A ring edge place is bad if that edge is sitting in the E slice.
- A ring corner place is bad if that corner is misoriented, wherever it is. To point it, look at which of its two side colours shows on the U or D face: it points at the edge place of its U/D colour and the *other* side colour. That is wen's tracing read the other way round: the corner's two stickers on the side faces are its U/D colour and that other colour.
- The holes are the E-slice edges sitting in the U or D layer. For the hole number look at what the two of them share: an R/L colour (red or orange) is 0, nothing is 1, an F/B colour (green or blue) is 2.

```
white-red edge in the E slice                   // UR is bad
white-red-green corner, green on the U/D face   // UFR points at UR: a pair
white-blue-red corner, red on the U/D face      // UBR points at UB, away from UR: a fake pair
```

This gave both rings and the holes of the inverse, place for place, on {= n(TRR('both rings', 'uniform')[1]) =} of {= n(TRR('both rings', 'uniform')[0]) =} uniform cubes and {= n(TRR('both rings', '4c4e')[1]) =} of {= n(TRR('both rings', '4c4e')[0]) =} 4c4e cubes, and the half turns to two bars counted from it matched on all {= n(TRR('half turns', '4c4e')[1]) =} of the 4c4e ones. The control, pointing each corner at the other edge place, was right on {= n(TRR('control', 'uniform')[1]) =} of {= n(TRR('control', 'uniform')[0]) =}.

**Edge ARM without changing axes** is Jay's reading ([[T4389]]): "On the general topic of DRM/ARM combos, I figured out an easy way to NISS predict an axis without actually looking at the other axis", and then "For green front EO as an example, you can note the number of misoriented corners with red/orange on top as being not in AR, and the unsolved belt edges (edges in E that belong in U) with green/blue on RL as being not in AR". The first half is the corner count of the table, the second the edge count. Why it works: the inverse's bad U/D edges are, piece for piece, the U/D edges sitting in the E slice on normal. One whose other colour is green or blue (F/B EO) belongs in the M slice, so it is an edge in M on the inverse. So for 4c4e, *edge ARM 1* on the switched case means exactly one of the two U/D edges in the E slice has a green/blue sticker, and you read it off the edges you trace for pairs anyway.

**Side pairs.** The 4c2e card needs side pairs on the inverse. The tracing is rodneykinney's, in *Domino Setup Theory*: "To predict side pairs on the inverse, look at the bad edges on the top/bottom faces. If there is also a bad corner showing the same color on the U/D face, then the inverse will have a side pair." (Jay calls it "Rodney's top pair and side pair prediction", [[T178]].) Jay said why side pairs matter: "Side pair stats would be valuable simply because NISS EO's don't destroy them unlike top pairs" ([[D7497]]). The doc's sentence names only the colour on the U/D face, and its picture (captioned "Inverse will have a side pair") shows the corner carrying both of the edge's colours. With that written in, the tracing in the table mirrors pair tracing: the E-slice edges that are out of the E slice (in U or D) play the edge, and the corner must carry both of the edge's colours and show its green or blue sticker on U or D. The control, the same count with the red or orange sticker on U or D, gives the right number on only {= n(TRACE('control', 'uniform')[1]) =} of {= n(TRACE('control', 'uniform')[0]) =} cubes, so the colour test is the whole rule.

The side-pair, edge-ARM and paired-edge readings also held on {= n(TRACE('side pairs', '4c2e')[0]) =} cubes drawn from 4c2e and {= n(TRACE('side pairs', '4c4e')[0]) =} from 4c4e. They're checked by sampling, not proved.
