# When to switch on an EO: the numbers (study 6)

Generated by `src/summary.py` from the files in `out/`. Setting: F/B EO solved, DR on U/D, EO kept (turns U D R L F2 B2), linear, one DR axis unless an option says otherwise; a state is a coset (twists of the 8 corners, places of the 4 E-slice edges), 1,082,565 of them. "Sub-7" = DR in at most 6. The rules are read on the state after the switch (the inverse of the post-EO+RZP state). Populations: P1 uniform (exact); P2 states reached by an RZP of length 1-6 from a uniform EO-solved state (exact counting); P3 real attempts on 6,000 uniformly random scrambles (`eodrtools mkscr 6000 6006`): every EO of at most 6 turns on each of the 3 axes, on the normal and the inverse scramble, each of its 2 DR axes, every RZP after it, total length 4, 5 or 6, the first time the path reaches its DRM: 121,527,134 attempts (about 20,000 per scramble, so errors on P3 come from 20 batches of 300 scrambles, not from the attempt count).

## Most striking findings

1. **After a switch, the RZP bias is gone.** Given its twist counts and its number of E-slice edges out, the switched state is uniform: the solver's choice of EO and RZP only sees cosets, and the inverse of a cube whose coset is fixed is uniform over its class. For every DRM except 6cXe and 8cXe the class mix cannot matter (one class, or two mirror-image classes), so every exhaustive table of uniform states is exactly right for switched RZPs. torchlight's bias (T4250) is real on the same side (4c4e sub-7 0.192 uniform, 0.183 for the end state of a 5-move RZP) and absent after the switch (0.192). Tested on the 121,527,134 sampled attempts: worst |z| 2.63 over 36 (DRM, length) cells.
2. **Count paired edges, not pairs (4c4e).** Of the two bad U/D-layer edges after the switch: neither paired 4.3% sub-7 (30% of 4c4e), both paired 59.9% (15%). Two pairs as a bar on one edge are 26.6%, two pairs on the two edges 53.9%; this holds in every ARM (e.g. AR-21: 44.2% vs 74.3%). "Switch if both bad edges have a pair" switches 15.5% of 4c4e at 59.9% sub-7; "2+ pairs" switches 26.0% at 46.4%.
3. **Add one look at the slices.** With exactly one paired edge, the case is 9.8% sub-7 when the two bad edges are in one slice (both in M or both in S) and 20.0% otherwise. The 6-cell rule (paired edges 0/1/2 x same slice) gives a verdict (under 10% or over 50%) on 67.5% of 4c4e states, against 33% for the pair count and 31% for the full ARM code.
4. **4c2e: pairs + side pairs/2 (Rodney's "1.5+ pairs") is the rule.** 1.5 or more: 57.0% sub-7 on 22.3% of 4c2e; 0 or 0.5: 1.5% and 8.1%; only "1" (15.7%) is in between. It gives a verdict on 75% of 4c2e states and holds 68% of the sub-7 ones in its switch group.
5. **7c8e: pairs alone is close to optimal among cheap features** (3 or fewer: under 4%; 5-6: 59%); ARM adds a little (strict separation 0.66 -> 0.69 with 36 cells).
6. **The trackers on the server are right.** The NISS predictors (ARM axis swap, R/L stickers on U/D = corner ARM on inverse, the corner-case flow chart of the DR 4c4e Theory doc, pair tracing) were each exact on 20,000 random cubes. Fanti's rule figures are any-turn optimal (F/B quarter turns allowed), not EO-kept; the differences are about a point at most.
7. **After a switch, the last turns are not free.** The direction of the RZP's last quarter turn does not change the switched case at all (a DR-group premove keeps the coset; checked on 114102 of 114102 pairs of example attempts), and inverting the EO's last turn only adds 2 points for 4c4e (19.1% -> 21.2%), against 9 points without a switch. The second DR axis adds 6 (25.0%).
8. **Low-rate DRMs have no cheap rescue.** For 4c6e, 3c4e, 3c6e, 5c4e, 5c6e and 6c6e no rule of up to 3 traceable features puts more than 23.9% of their sub-7 states into a cell above 50%; discarding them is right.

Why the last RZP turn is useless after a switch: the switched state is (S E R)^-1 = R^-1 E^-1 S^-1, so the RZP's last turn becomes the first premove on the inverse, and a premove from the DR group (U, D, R2, L2, F2, B2; R and R' differ by R2) does not change which pieces are twisted or which places hold E-slice pieces relative to their homes, i.e. the coset. The EO's last turn only matters through the RZP after it (with no RZP it is useless too).

## 7. Not done, and uncertain

- Rodney's "findable sub-7" (DRM Subsets, trees): not reproduced; it classifies the one optimal solution his generator kept per state, so it depends on search order.
- No Lean certificate of the per-feature tables (SPEC "Checks"): the tables are checked against eo-to-dr's exact table, nissy, Rodney's own code and the sticker model instead.
- E15 (T10704, `F B' U R2 U`): in the only frame where both sides are 4c2e, both have 2 pairs (side pairs 2 vs 0); the claim "2 pairs on normal, 0 on inverse" could not be matched to any frame.
- Evan's C13 pair rates (2 and 3 pairs) are off by 0.017-0.032 from the exact values with no explanation found.
- P3 counts attempts, not scrambles: a solver sees one attempt at a time, and the about 20,000 attempts per scramble are strongly dependent; the uniformity test uses batch errors for that reason. P3 has no notion of which EOs a solver finds or tries first; every EO of at most 6 counts equally.
- Feature costs ("2 s", "5 s") in `src/search.py` are rough guesses from the server quotes (Yura D14243: pairs + ARM about 5 s for 4c4e), not measured.
- The doc 320 flow chart and the pair-tracing rule are checked by sampling (20,000 cubes each), not proved.
