## 2. What was asked

The questions this study set out to answer:

- *How long should be spent on each step?* Section 6.
- *Are there situations where depth-first searching is helpful?* Section 7.
- *Is it useful to have an adaptive time budgeting strategy that changes based on features of the scramble?* Section 8.
- *What's the best "safety" approach to get at most 22, at risk of missing something better?* Section 9.
- *How does the average result scale as a function of different time budgeting decisions?* Section 10.
- *Top-level humans average around 20-21 moves, so target that or better.* Section 5 measures where the top is: the five best official means since 2024 average 21.9, and the simulated baseline sits next to them. Section 10 says how far faster steps and sharper reads take it.

Questions from the server that the same runs answer:

- JTuu: "when you have let's say 21 safety with 20 mins or so to look for better, anyone have advice for knowing when you should squeeze the 21 harder vs widen your search and prune very aggressively? I feel like I frequently do the wrong one" ([[D9642]]), unanswered in the thread. Section 9.
- Lichi Fang: "In scrambles where you have several mid DRs at 30 minutes (for example a 11+2c3, a 10+4c3, a 10+2c4, and a 11+2b4), is it in general better to start finishing DRs sooner or later? (Or like what's the general philosophy of a good time management?)" ([[D8035]]). Yura Riabov answered "Sooner in this case, cause you'd want to check briefly 11s, most simple paths of 4c3 and go grinding 2c4" ([[D8036]]). Sections 6 and 8.
- Tzak: "at what time do people settle and work on some 2c4 vs trying for more drs" ([[D12301]]). Section 6.
- DRCubeur: "Is stopping checking EOs at a certain time even if I’m not finished worth it?" ([[D16707]]). Section 6.
- rodneykinney's "cost-optimized FMC search strategy" ([[T6582]]) and Tom Nelson's proposal to simulate solves ([[D3238]]): this page is a first version of both, for the DR method.
