HTR cases

Every HTR case of a DR on U/D, up to rotations, mirrors and the E slice: 1,272 cases, grouped by subset, then bad edges, then the fewest moves to HTR. On each cube the bad pieces are solid red and the rest is glass. Click a case for every HTR of 8 moves or less (or the shortest ones, when none is that short). Drag a cube to turn it.

What counts as a case, and how the HTRs are listed
  • Bad pieces. A corner is bad when it sits in the other tetrad (where no half turn can take it); an edge is bad when an M-slice edge (UF UB DF DB) sits on an S-slice place (UL UR DL DR), or the other way. They are counted in the frame with few of them, as subsets are named: 2c3 2e and 6c3 6e are one case.
  • One case. Everything that decides which move sequences take a DR to HTR: two DRs are the same case when exactly the same sequences take them to HTR. Rotations and mirrors that keep U/D on top and bottom count as the same case (the picture shows one of them), and so does the E slice: turning it makes every bad piece good and every good one bad (2c3 2e becomes 6c3 6e). Both are the same number of moves from HTR, for every case but HTR itself (case 1 below), and they have the same HTRs, with the last quarter turn on the other layer (U for D, D for U). Each case has a switch to show the other one.
  • Lookalikes. Several cases have the same bad pieces: they look the same here and on the cube. Each case lists its lookalikes, and has a corner test: a shortest sequence that puts its corners in HTR. On any of its lookalikes the same sequence leaves fake HTR. Every 4b2 and 2c3 case also carries the label solvers tell such lookalikes apart by:
    • 4b2: no parity / parity. Set the corners up for a two-quarter-turn trigger (Q X2 Q) with half turns, counting every U2 or D2 done while the bad corners are split 3/1 between U and D. An even count and the trigger gives real HTR: no parity. An odd count: parity.
    • 2c3: clockwise / anticlockwise. From the position shown, the quarter turn (U or D, each seen from its own face) that brings it to 4b2 with no parity. Every half turn done before it flips the direction.
    The 4b2 rule is the one in the Hitchhiker's Guide to HTR (Yurii Riabov, July 2024, "How to solve 4b2"). The 2c3 one puts together the guide's "How to solve 2c3" (with parity after the quarter turn, turn the other way) and count quackula's "every half turn you do on a 2c3 changes the qt direction" (3 Sep 2026). Both give one answer on every route, the guide's own examples come out as it says, and the two 4b2 lookalikes of each shape are one with parity and one without.
  • Pairs a U2 apart. Some cases are another case after U2, and on both a shortest HTR starts with a U quarter turn: U on one is U′ on the other (U2 U′ is U), so after that turn they are the same case. There are 192 such pairs, like cases 310 and 311. Each case names its pair, and the switch above the list shows every pair as one entry (1,080 entries instead of 1,272). The symmetries swap U and D, so pairs a D2 apart are among them.
  • The HTRs listed. DR moves only (U, D, R2, L2, F2, B2), turns of opposite faces in the order U D, R L, F B, and none that is in HTR before its end, even with the E slice a quarter turn off (from there the rest only turns the slice back), or that has a half turn among its last turns (that half turn could go last, where it does nothing for HTR). So each one ends in a single quarter turn that can go either way: U and U′ both reach HTR, and only the clockwise one is written. HTRs that are the same up to the E slice are written once, the shortest of them: with the E slice ignored, a D turn is the U turn the same way with the cube turned a quarter with it, so the turns after it are on the next faces round (R to F, F to L, L to B, B to R). Case 310's U R2 U R2 U, U R2 D B2 D, D B2 U B2 D and D B2 D L2 U are one HTR. That leaves about one HTR in fifteen: 27,888 over all the cases. Case 1, HTR up to the E slice, lists only U D.
  • For other programs. htr-cases.txt: a shortest generator of every case (DR moves that make it from a solved cube, as drawn), with its subset, edges, moves, label and bad pieces, numbered as here.
