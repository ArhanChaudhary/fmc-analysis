# Which HTR routes to try from a DR: corrections

*Goes with the write-up on which HTR routes to try from a DR, but reads on its own. The write-up has the measurements and the tables.*

November 2026. A date in brackets is a link to that message on the FMC solvers Discord (#fmc-theory, #fmc-discussion or #non-dr-discussion).

**What everything below is measured against:**

- **The exact optimal finish of every DR state** (all 19,508,428,800, from the first study, proved in Lean there), and the shortest linear HTR of every one of the 29,400 HTR cosets.
- **25,000 uniformly random DR states per subset × edges** (1,200,000 in all), each solved exactly: every optimal and +1 finish, every NISS reading, the shortest HTR on each side and with NISS, every reduction to 1q.

Every scramble and move sequence below was replayed on a sticker-level cube. Each item also says how much of its example is checked in Lean (the study's folder lean/FakeBound: Corrections/Examples.lean and the FakeBound files); the rest is computed, not proved. These are the statements the numbers go against; most of what was said holds up, and that is in the write-up's section 11.

---

**a. "so yeah, should be 1/6"** (Yura Riabov, [6 Jun 2024](https://discord.com/channels/1007840975726575667/1007841335434285126/1248337792673779803), on how much turning the last DR move the other way improves the finish).
Over all DRs the better of X and X·R2 is 0.325 moves shorter on average: X·R2 is one move shorter on 32.5% of DRs and never more than one, so the gain is about 1/3 of a move, twice the estimate. Yura Riabov called the estimate "very rough" and said "i doubt it will be more than a half move difference with 1 move inverted" ([6 Jun 2024](https://discord.com/channels/1007840975726575667/1007841335434285126/1248372741602545674)); that ceiling holds. The first study had the exact third.
Example: think of `R2 D R2 F2 R2 D L2 D B2 R2 D2 B2 U` as the end of a DR on U/D whose last move was a quarter turn of R (say R); turning that move the other way (R') gives the same state followed by R2. As written the 4b2 2e is 13 optimal (`U' B2 D2 R2 B2 D' L2 D' R2 F2 R2 D' R2`); with R2 appended, `R2 D R2 F2 R2 D L2 D B2 R2 D2 B2 U R2`, it is 12: `B2 U' R2 L2 D L2 U L2 U2 R2 U' F2`. *Lean-checked*: both optima, both finishes and the subset (`dr_optima_correct`, `dr_solutions_valid`, `labels_htr_routes`). The share and the mean gain are counted over the first study's exact table, not proved in Lean.

**b. "Interesting that it seems 4qt 4C0E is the only HTR subset that is guaranteed 11 moves without NISS"** (Jay, [29 Jan 2023](https://discord.com/channels/1007840975726575667/1007841335434285126/1069455768782385294)).
Over all 29,400 HTR cosets: 4c4 0e ("4qt 4C0E") has linear HTRs of 10 moves, and the one subset whose shortest linear HTR is always 11 or more is 0c4 8e (fake HTR corners with 8 bad edges). Lewis Isom noticed the 10 in Aedan Bryant's sheet in 2024 ([23 Jan 2024](https://discord.com/channels/1007840975726575667/1007841335434285126/1199494282223038485): "also under 4qt 4c0e the trigger given is 11 moves but stats say 10 is possible").
Example: `D F2 R2 U2 B2 U' F2 U' F2 U L2 B2 U' R2 U'` is a 4c4 0e; nissy finds the linear HTR `(D R2 U R2 B2 U2 B2 U B2 U) (10)` on the inverse. *Lean-checked*, all of it: the subset, the 10-move linear HTR on the inverse and that the printed one is valid, and that 0c4 8e is the only subset × edges whose shortest linear HTR is never below 11 (`htr_4c4`).

**c. "No, the maximum it takes is 12 moves linearly, and 11 moves using NISS."** (Lewis Isom, [23 Jan 2024](https://discord.com/channels/1007840975726575667/1007841335434285126/1199490255921877022), answering whether DR to HTR takes at most 9.)
Both are one short: 0c3 4e and 4c4 0e have HTR cosets whose shortest linear HTR is 13, and in the sample some DRs need 13 on both sides and 12 with NISS. Daichi had the 13 right later ([14 May 2026](https://discord.com/channels/1007840975726575667/1007841335434285126/1504408526444040192): "0c3 4e and 4c4 0e have 13 move optimal HTR cases").
Example: the 0c3 4e `B2 U B2 R2 D' L2 U B2 D' F2 D' L2 F2 U' R2 U` (16 optimal). nissy's shortest linear HTR on either side: `U R2 U B2 U' B2 U F2 U' R2 U B2 U (13)`; with NISS: `U R2 U2 F2 U' F2 R2 U (B2 U L2 U) (12)`. *Lean-checked*: the optimum, the subset, the 13-move shortest linear HTR on each side, and that both printed HTRs are valid (`dr_optima_correct`, `htr_13`). That no NISS HTR of 11 exists is from nissy and the study's search, not Lean.

**d. "this isn't the worst practice but it's pretty rare for switching after doing more than 2 moves to actually be useful, unless you're variationmaxing"** (Evan, [2 May 2025](https://discord.com/channels/1007840975726575667/1007841273111117924/1368063770576027648)).
The optimal through its shortest HTR needs a switch after more than 2 moves on 28.2% of DRs (sampled, 25,000 per subset × edges): take the shortest HTR that any reading of any optimal finish reaches; every reading that reaches it switches after 3 or more moves. That is an upper bound on how often a late switch is needed for the optimal: on some of these DRs another optimal finish, with a longer HTR, may be readable linearly or with an early switch, which is not measured here. Evan named exceptions in the same thread ("the b side of 4c3", "4c4 and 0c4 ofc but those are pretty rare", [3 May 2025](https://discord.com/channels/1007840975726575667/1007841273111117924/1368159205072175145), [3 May 2025](https://discord.com/channels/1007840975726575667/1007841273111117924/1368159998848274462)); leaving out all of 4c3, 4c4 and 0c4 it is still 24.0%: 17.9% of 2c3, 16.1% of 4b2, 24.1% of 2c4, 43.5% of 2c5. Yura Riabov's line, "More like 3 imo" ([3 May 2025](https://discord.com/channels/1007840975726575667/1007841273111117924/1368155824371007488)), fits much better: after 4 or more moves it is 5.5% of 2c3 and 4.9% of 4b2, large only on 0c4, 4c4 and 2c5. Yura Riabov added "i almost never do this but i think it is useful" and "i really don't like switching after more than 2 moves or for badish cases, which is why i'm worse at finishes than i could be" ([3 May 2025](https://discord.com/channels/1007840975726575667/1007841273111117924/1368162064148725770), [3 May 2025](https://discord.com/channels/1007840975726575667/1007841273111117924/1368162218579071158)).
Example: the 2c3 2e `F2 U' L2 U' F2 R2 F2 L2 U F2 D2 B2` has a single optimal finish, `B2 D2 F2 U' L2 F2 R2 F2 U L2 U F2` (12). Its shortest HTR in any reading is 8 moves, and every reading with that HTR switches after 4 moves: `B2 D2 F2 U'` on normal, then `(F2 U' L2 U' F2 R2 F2 L2)` on inverse. With a single optimal finish the upper bound is exact: here the late switch is needed. *Lean-checked*: the optimum, the finish and the subset (`dr_optima_correct`, `dr_solutions_valid`, `labels_htr_routes`). That it is the only optimal finish, and the HTR lengths of its readings, come from the study's enumeration (nissy also finds one optimal), not Lean.

**e. "I like to check all sub-5 reductions. This can be done super quick and gets optimal like 75% of the time, and it gets optimal +1 the rest of the time"** (Levi Gibson, [28 Nov 2024](https://discord.com/channels/1007840975726575667/1007841273111117924/1311670582601060354); "sub-5" is "4 or less", [29 Nov 2024](https://discord.com/channels/1007840975726575667/1007841273111117924/1312051485760163880)).
Just before, Levi Gibson wrote "for the 4a2 subset I use full DR Bounding" ([28 Nov 2024](https://discord.com/channels/1007840975726575667/1007841273111117924/1311669869900464138)), and a few minutes later "I don't use it fully for any subsets other than 4a2 and 2c3" ([28 Nov 2024](https://discord.com/channels/1007840975726575667/1007841273111117924/1311671487379279872)). Write every reduction to 1q of at most 4 moves, on normal, on inverse and split by NISS, and finish each reduced state optimally, with any number of quarter turns (sampled, 4,000 states per subset × edges). On 4a2 the 75% holds: the optimal on 76.6% (207 reductions on average). "+1 the rest of the time" does not: a finish within +1 on 93.6%. On 2c3 both fall short: the optimal on 48.1% (52.1% of 2c3 2e, 46.2% of 2c3 4e; 140 reductions on average) and within +1 on 78.8%. On 2c3 2e and 4e Levi Gibson adds triggers: "For 2c3 2e and 4e I do full DR Bounding + checking the 5-move triggers, and that seems to catch everything" ([28 Nov 2024](https://discord.com/channels/1007840975726575667/1007841273111117924/1311674286389334026)). The triggers are not measured here, so this corrects the share for bounding alone. Levi Gibson's later answer to "Would it be around 80 percent?" for 2c3 was a guess ("yeah something like that I'd guess", [29 Nov 2024](https://discord.com/channels/1007840975726575667/1007841273111117924/1312051081831780434)), with the advice to check "a bunch of half turns into a 5-move trigger" as well ([29 Nov 2024](https://discord.com/channels/1007840975726575667/1007841273111117924/1312051209158525018)).
Example: the 2c3 2e `R2 B2 D2 B2 D U2 R2 U L2 F2 U' B2` is 12 optimal. All 168 different states reached by a reduction to 4a1 4e of at most 4 moves were enumerated on the sticker model and finished optimally from the exact table: the best total is 14, optimal + 2. The optimal needs a 5-move reduction, `B2 U F2 L2 U'` on normal, then `R2 U2 D' B2 D2 B2 R2`. *Lean-checked*: the optimum and the subset (`dr_optima_correct`, `labels_htr_routes`). The enumeration of the reductions and their finishes is computed, not Lean.

**f. FakeMMAP's reduction bound and its multi-qt lower bounds** ([8 Apr 2026](https://discord.com/channels/1007840975726575667/1007841335434285126/1491531409959948369): "R is the max of the following", five families of terms, the sums "over all i's such that i, i+1 are both in S"; [8 Apr 2026](https://discord.com/channels/1007840975726575667/1007841335434285126/1491533155201716475): "M \geq \frac{|S|(R'+1)-N+\sum_{i edge} \alpha_i}{|S|-1}").
Of the lower bounds FakeMMAP wrote "I don't know how useful these bounds really are, I have to put in the numbers to see if there are situations where the typical bound is M \geq 11, rather than M \geq 7 lol." ([8 Apr 2026](https://discord.com/channels/1007840975726575667/1007841335434285126/1491533429198553178)); this is that test (the write-up's section 9 gives the typical values). R as written is safe to use: the largest term is never too short, proved for every finish (`Rmax_sound`, lean/FakeBound/FakeBound/Formula.lean), but only because term 1, about M − 1, is among the terms, so it is loose. Two things in the formulas are wrong, each proved in Lean with a counterexample.
*The α index, in both messages.* Windows i and i+1 share section i+1, so the section counted twice is α_{i+1}, not α_i (`cover_sum`, FakeBound/Finish.lean). In the lower bounds: over 1,914,809 sampled (state, corner path) cases at the optimum, the bounds as written were wrong in 923: 904 times the first bound, 19 times the one with B. With α_{i+1} they were never wrong (0 cases), and they are proved valid for every finish (`lower_free`, `lower_A`, `lower_B`, `lower_C`, FakeBound/Finish.lean). In the terms of the upper bound the same index makes a term too short (`alpha_i_fails`, FakeBound/Counter.lean): on the path 4b2 > 4a3 > 4b2 > 4a1 > 0c0, a 14-move finish with sections 2, 1, 3, 1, 3 needs a 9-move reduction, but the smallest term with α_i is 8. Under "max" that does no harm; under the smallest-term reading below it does.
Example for the index: the 0c4 0e `D2 F2 L2 U' B2 U' L2 U2 B2 U' L2 D R2 D2` is 14 optimal by `D2 R2 D' L2 U B2 U2 L2 U B2 U L2 F2 D2`, corner path 0c4 > 4a3 > 4b2 > 4a1 > HTR, N = 4, α = 0, 1, 3, 1, 0. Its sections of half turns are 2, 1, 3, 1, 3, its 1q reductions 10, 9, 9, 9, so all reductions shorter than R' = 9 fail. With S = {0, 2, 3} the bound as written gives M ≥ 14.5, so 15, with α_{i+1} 13.5, so 14, the optimum. *Lean-checked end to end*: the optimum, the finish, the subset, the corner path, α, the sections, the reductions and both inequalities (`dr_optima_correct`, `dr_solutions_valid`, `labels_htr_routes`, `index_example`, `index_example_arith`, Corrections/Examples.lean).
*Term 2's signs, when R is the smallest term.* Every term is an upper bound on the reduction a finish needs, so the bound the derivation gives is the smallest term, and with α_{i+1} and term 2 as derived it is sound for every finish (`Rmin_sound`, FakeBound/Formula.lean; it is not always the exact worst case, `Rmin_not_tight`). Term 2 as printed, "\frac{(|S|-1)M-(N-|S|)+\sum_{i edge} \alpha_i}{|S|}", has both signs flipped: the derivation gives ((|S|−1)M + (N−|S|) − Σα)/|S|, and so does FakeMMAP's own first lower bound ([8 Apr 2026](https://discord.com/channels/1007840975726575667/1007841335434285126/1491533155201716475)) solved for R'. With the printed term 2 the smallest term is too short: on a 10-move finish with sections 0, 3, 0, 4 (path 4a1 > 0c0 > 4a1 > 0c0) the reductions are 6, 6, 5, but printed term 2 with S = {0, 2} gives 4, whatever A, B and C are, and still 4 with the printed α_i as well (`printed_term2_fails`, `literal_min_fails`, FakeBound/Counter.lean). In the sample it is too short on 9.2% of cases at the optimum.
Example for the signs: the 0c3 2e `F2 D2 R2 D' R2 U2 B2 D' L2 U2 F2 U L2 R2` is 14 optimal by `R2 L2 U' F2 U2 L2 D B2 U2 R2 D R2 D2 F2`, and every optimal finish takes the path 0c3 > 4a2 > 4a1 > HTR (N = 3, α = 0, 3, 3, 0). Its sections are 2, 3, 3, 3, so its 1q reductions are 8, 7, 7 and a 7-move reduction is needed. Term 2 as printed with S = {0, 2} gives 6.5, so 6; derived, 7.5, so 7. *Lean-checked*: the arithmetic from the sections on (`witness_0c3`). The state's optimum and its paths come from the exact table and the study's enumeration, not Lean.
*An earlier version of this entry (until 12 Nov 2026) also said that C, "the shortest possible HTR in the subset", must be the shortest NISS HTR. That was wrong: C bounds the middle of the finish, a linear HTR of a DR in the same subset (half turns keep the subset, proved in Lean: `labels_keep`), so FakeMMAP's linear C is right (section 9 of the write-up). The mistake was to bound the finish minus one middle section instead, which is a NISS HTR of the DR itself: the 0c0 2e `F2 U' B2 R2 B2 U R2` is 7 optimal by `R2 U' B2 R2 B2 U F2`, and without its middle `B2 R2 B2` it leaves `R2 U'` on normal and `(F2 U')` on inverse, a 4-move NISS HTR, below the subset's shortest linear HTR of 5 (Lean-checked: `C_example`, `dr_optima_correct`).*
