# Which HTR routes to try from a DR: corrections

*Goes with the write-up on which HTR routes to try from a DR, but reads on its own. The write-up has the measurements and the tables.*

November 2026. A date in brackets is a link to that message on the FMC solvers Discord (#fmc-theory, #fmc-discussion or #non-dr-discussion).

**What everything below is measured against:**

- **The exact optimal finish of every DR state** (all 19,508,428,800, from the first study, proved in Lean there), and the shortest linear HTR of every one of the 29,400 HTR cosets.
- **25,000 uniformly random DR states per subset × edges** (1,200,000 in all), each solved exactly: every optimal and +1 finish, every NISS reading, the shortest HTR on each side and with NISS, every reduction to 1q.

Every scramble and move sequence below was replayed on a sticker-level cube. These are the statements the numbers go against; most of what was said holds up, and that is in the write-up's section 11.

---

**a. "so yeah, should be 1/6"** (Yura Riabov, [6 Jun 2024](https://discord.com/channels/1007840975726575667/1007841335434285126/1248337792673779803), on how much turning the last DR move the other way improves the finish).
Over all DRs the better of X and X·R2 is 0.325 moves shorter on average: X·R2 is one move shorter on 32.5% of DRs and never more than one, so the gain is about 1/3 of a move, twice the estimate. Yura Riabov called the estimate "very rough" and said "i doubt it will be more than a half move difference with 1 move inverted" ([6 Jun 2024](https://discord.com/channels/1007840975726575667/1007841335434285126/1248372741602545674)); that ceiling holds. The first study had the exact third.
Example: think of `R2 D R2 F2 R2 D L2 D B2 R2 D2 B2 U` as the end of a DR on U/D whose last move was a quarter turn of R (say R); turning that move the other way (R') gives the same state followed by R2. As written the 4b2 2e is 13 optimal (`U' B2 D2 R2 B2 D' L2 D' R2 F2 R2 D' R2`); with R2 appended, `R2 D R2 F2 R2 D L2 D B2 R2 D2 B2 U R2`, it is 12: `B2 U' R2 L2 D L2 U L2 U2 R2 U' F2`.

**b. "Interesting that it seems 4qt 4C0E is the only HTR subset that is guaranteed 11 moves without NISS"** (Jay, [29 Jan 2023](https://discord.com/channels/1007840975726575667/1007841335434285126/1069455768782385294)).
Over all 29,400 HTR cosets: 4c4 0e ("4qt 4C0E") has linear HTRs of 10 moves, and the one subset whose shortest linear HTR is always 11 or more is 0c4 8e (fake HTR corners with 8 bad edges). Lewis Isom noticed the 10 in Aedan Bryant's sheet in 2024 ([23 Jan 2024](https://discord.com/channels/1007840975726575667/1007841335434285126/1199494282223038485): "also under 4qt 4c0e the trigger given is 11 moves but stats say 10 is possible").
Example: `D F2 R2 U2 B2 U' F2 U' F2 U L2 B2 U' R2 U'` is a 4c4 0e; nissy finds the linear HTR `(D R2 U R2 B2 U2 B2 U B2 U) (10)` on the inverse.

**c. "No, the maximum it takes is 12 moves linearly, and 11 moves using NISS."** (Lewis Isom, [23 Jan 2024](https://discord.com/channels/1007840975726575667/1007841335434285126/1199490255921877022), answering whether DR to HTR takes at most 9.)
Both are one short: 0c3 4e and 4c4 0e have HTR cosets whose shortest linear HTR is 13, and in the sample some DRs need 13 on both sides and 12 with NISS. Daichi had the 13 right later ([14 May 2026](https://discord.com/channels/1007840975726575667/1007841335434285126/1504408526444040192): "0c3 4e and 4c4 0e have 13 move optimal HTR cases").
Example: the 0c3 4e `B2 U B2 R2 D' L2 U B2 D' F2 D' L2 F2 U' R2 U` (16 optimal). nissy's shortest linear HTR on either side: `U R2 U B2 U' B2 U F2 U' R2 U B2 U (13)`; with NISS: `U R2 U2 F2 U' F2 R2 U (B2 U L2 U) (12)`.

**d. "this isn't the worst practice but it's pretty rare for switching after doing more than 2 moves to actually be useful, unless you're variationmaxing"** (Evan, [2 May 2025](https://discord.com/channels/1007840975726575667/1007841273111117924/1368063770576027648)).
For the shortest HTR that any optimal finish uses, every such reading switches after 3 or more moves on 28.2% of DRs (sampled, 25,000 per subset × edges). Evan named exceptions in the same thread ("the b side of 4c3", "4c4 and 0c4 ofc but those are pretty rare", [3 May 2025](https://discord.com/channels/1007840975726575667/1007841273111117924/1368159205072175145), [3 May 2025](https://discord.com/channels/1007840975726575667/1007841273111117924/1368159998848274462)); leaving out all of 4c3, 4c4 and 0c4 it is still 24.0%: 17.9% of 2c3, 16.1% of 4b2, 24.1% of 2c4, 43.5% of 2c5. Yura Riabov's line, "More like 3 imo" ([3 May 2025](https://discord.com/channels/1007840975726575667/1007841273111117924/1368155824371007488)), fits much better: after 4 or more moves it is 5.5% of 2c3 and 4.9% of 4b2, large only on 0c4, 4c4 and 2c5. Yura Riabov added "i almost never do this but i think it is useful" and "i really don't like switching after more than 2 moves or for badish cases, which is why i'm worse at finishes than i could be" ([3 May 2025](https://discord.com/channels/1007840975726575667/1007841273111117924/1368162064148725770), [3 May 2025](https://discord.com/channels/1007840975726575667/1007841273111117924/1368162218579071158)).
Example: the 2c3 2e `F2 U' L2 U' F2 R2 F2 L2 U F2 D2 B2` has a single optimal finish, `B2 D2 F2 U' L2 F2 R2 F2 U L2 U F2` (12). Its shortest HTR in any reading is 8 moves, and every reading with that HTR switches after 4 moves: `B2 D2 F2 U'` on normal, then `(F2 U' L2 U' F2 R2 F2 L2)` on inverse.

**e. "I like to check all sub-5 reductions. This can be done super quick and gets optimal like 75% of the time, and it gets optimal +1 the rest of the time"** (Levi Gibson, [28 Nov 2024](https://discord.com/channels/1007840975726575667/1007841273111117924/1311670582601060354); "sub-5" is "4 or less", [29 Nov 2024](https://discord.com/channels/1007840975726575667/1007841273111117924/1312051485760163880)).
Writing every reduction to 1q of at most 4 moves, on normal, on inverse and split by NISS, and finishing each reduced state optimally, finds the optimal on 48.1% of 2c3 (52.1% of 2c3 2e, 46.2% of 2c3 4e) and a finish within +1 on 78.8% (sampled, 4,000 states per subset × edges; 140 reductions on average). On no subset does it reach 75% (the best is 0c0, 91.5%). Levi Gibson used the method mainly on 2c3 and 4a2 ("I don't use it fully for any subsets other than 4a2 and 2c3", [28 Nov 2024](https://discord.com/channels/1007840975726575667/1007841273111117924/1311671487379279872)), on 4a2 it is 76.6%, and on 2c3 2e and 4e together with triggers: "For 2c3 2e and 4e I do full DR Bounding + checking the 5-move triggers, and that seems to catch everything" ([28 Nov 2024](https://discord.com/channels/1007840975726575667/1007841273111117924/1311674286389334026)); the triggers are not measured here, so this corrects the share for bounding alone. Levi Gibson's later answer to "Would it be around 80 percent?" for 2c3 was a guess ("yeah something like that I'd guess", [29 Nov 2024](https://discord.com/channels/1007840975726575667/1007841273111117924/1312051081831780434)), with the advice to check "a bunch of half turns into a 5-move trigger" as well ([29 Nov 2024](https://discord.com/channels/1007840975726575667/1007841273111117924/1312051209158525018)).
Example: the 2c3 2e `R2 B2 D2 B2 D U2 R2 U L2 F2 U' B2` is 12 optimal. All 168 different states reached by a reduction to 4a1 4e of at most 4 moves were enumerated on the sticker model and finished optimally from the exact table: the best total is 14, optimal + 2. The optimal needs a 5-move reduction, `B2 U F2 L2 U'` on normal, then `R2 U2 D' B2 D2 B2 R2`.

**f. The multi-qt lower bounds of FakeMMAP's reduction bound** ([8 Apr 2026](https://discord.com/channels/1007840975726575667/1007841335434285126/1491533155201716475): "M \geq \frac{|S|(R'+1)-N+\sum_{i edge} \alpha_i}{|S|-1}", with the sum, as in [8 Apr 2026](https://discord.com/channels/1007840975726575667/1007841335434285126/1491531409959948369), "over all i's such that i, i+1 are both in S").
Of these lower bounds FakeMMAP wrote "I don't know how useful these bounds really are, I have to put in the numbers to see if there are situations where the typical bound is M \geq 11, rather than M \geq 7 lol." ([8 Apr 2026](https://discord.com/channels/1007840975726575667/1007841335434285126/1491533429198553178)); this is that test (the write-up's section 9 gives the typical values). Over 1,914,809 sampled (state, corner path) cases at the optimum, the bounds as written were wrong in 923: 904 times the first bound, 19 times the one with B. Windows i and i+1 share section i+1, so the section counted twice is α_{i+1}, not α_i. With α_{i+1} the bounds held in every case. (The upper bound R of [8 Apr 2026](https://discord.com/channels/1007840975726575667/1007841335434285126/1491531409959948369) was never too short.) A second point, for the derivation: C, "the shortest possible HTR in the subset" ([8 Apr 2026](https://discord.com/channels/1007840975726575667/1007841335434285126/1491531394667773962)), has to bound HTRs with a switch in the middle, so it must be the shortest NISS HTR, which can be below the linear values FakeMMAP gave ("so 2c3 2e would have C=5"); with the linear C, the exact version of the model is too short on 0.073% of cases (the looser formula as written still held).
Example for the index: the 0c4 0e `D2 F2 L2 U' B2 U' L2 U2 B2 U' L2 D R2 D2` is 14 optimal by `D2 R2 D' L2 U B2 U2 L2 U B2 U L2 F2 D2`, corner path 0c4 > 4a3 > 4b2 > 4a1 > HTR, N = 4, α = 0, 1, 3, 1, 0. Its sections of half turns are 2, 1, 3, 1, 3, its 1q reductions 10, 9, 9, 9, so all reductions shorter than R' = 9 fail. With S = {0, 2, 3} the bound as written gives M ≥ 14.5, so 15, with α_{i+1} 13.5, so 14, the optimum.
Example for C: the 0c0 2e `F2 U' B2 R2 B2 U R2` is 7 optimal by `R2 U' B2 R2 B2 U F2`, read as `R2 U'` on normal and `(F2 U' B2 R2 B2)` on inverse, which is in HTR after 4 moves; the shortest linear HTR anywhere in 0c0 2e is 5.
