# Which HTR routes to try from a DR: corrections

*Goes with the write-up on which HTR routes to try from a DR, but reads on its own. The write-up has the measurements and the tables.*

November 2026. A date in brackets is a link to that message on the FMC solvers Discord (#fmc-theory, #fmc-discussion or #non-dr-discussion).

**What everything below is measured against:**

- **The exact optimal finish of every DR state** (all 19,508,428,800, from the first study, proved in Lean there), and the shortest linear HTR of every one of the 29,400 HTR cosets.
- **{= n(25000) =} uniformly random DR states per subset × edges** ({= n(1200000) =} in all), each solved exactly: every optimal and +1 finish, every NISS reading, the shortest HTR on each side and with NISS, every reduction to 1q.

Every scramble and move sequence below was replayed on a sticker-level cube. These are the statements the numbers go against; most of what was said holds up, and that is in the write-up's section 11.

---

**a. "so yeah, should be 1/6"** (Yura Riabov, [[T4606]], on how much turning the last DR move the other way improves the finish).
Over all DRs the better of X and X·R2 is {= f3(Q5('all', 'mean_gain')) =} moves shorter on average: X·R2 is one move shorter on {= pc(Q5('all', 'share_variation_shorter')) =} of DRs and never more than one, so the gain is about 1/3 of a move, twice the estimate. Yura Riabov called the estimate "very rough" and said "i doubt it will be more than a half move difference with 1 move inverted" ([[T4610]]); that ceiling holds. The first study had the exact third.
Example: think of `{= WIT('W06', 'scramble') =}` as the end of a DR on U/D whose last move was a quarter turn of R (say R); turning that move the other way (R') gives the same state followed by R2. As written the 4b2 2e is {= WIT('W06', 'optimal') =} optimal (`{= WIT('W06', 'optimal_finish') =}`); with R2 appended, `{= WIT('W06', 'variation_scramble') =}`, it is {= WIT('W06', 'variation_optimal') =}: `{= WIT('W06', 'variation_finish') =}`.

**b. "Interesting that it seems 4qt 4C0E is the only HTR subset that is guaranteed 11 moves without NISS"** (Jay, [[T619]]).
Over all 29,400 HTR cosets: 4c4 0e ("4qt 4C0E") has linear HTRs of {= MINLIN('4c4 0e') =} moves, and the one subset whose shortest linear HTR is always {= MINLIN('0c4 8e') =} or more is 0c4 8e (fake HTR corners with 8 bad edges). Lewis Isom noticed the 10 in Aedan Bryant's sheet in 2024 ([[T4104]]: "also under 4qt 4c0e the trigger given is 11 moves but stats say 10 is possible").
Example: `{= WIT('W08', 'scramble') =}` is a 4c4 0e; nissy finds the linear HTR `{= WIT('W08', 'nissy_linear_htr') =}` on the inverse.

**c. "No, the maximum it takes is 12 moves linearly, and 11 moves using NISS."** (Lewis Isom, [[T4100]], answering whether DR to HTR takes at most 9.)
Both are one short: 0c3 4e and 4c4 0e have HTR cosets whose shortest linear HTR is {= MAXLIN('0c3 4e') =}, and in the sample some DRs need 13 on both sides and 12 with NISS. Daichi had the 13 right later ([[T11594]]: "0c3 4e and 4c4 0e have 13 move optimal HTR cases").
Example: the 0c3 4e `{= WXX('X2', 'scramble') =}` ({= WXX('X2', 'optimal') =} optimal). nissy's shortest linear HTR on either side: `{= WXX('X2', 'nissy_linear') =}`; with NISS: `{= WXX('X2', 'nissy_niss') =}`.

**d. "this isn't the worst practice but it's pretty rare for switching after doing more than 2 moves to actually be useful, unless you're variationmaxing"** (Evan, [[D11196]]).
For the shortest HTR that any optimal finish uses, every such reading switches after 3 or more moves on {= pc(C11('all', 'shortest_htr_needs_switch_after_gt2')) =} of DRs (sampled, {= n(25000) =} per subset × edges). Evan named exceptions in the same thread ("the b side of 4c3", "4c4 and 0c4 ofc but those are pretty rare", [[D11237]], [[D11238]]); leaving out all of 4c3, 4c4 and 0c4 it is still {= pc(E['late_excl_gt2']) =}: {= pc(C11('2c3', 'shortest_htr_needs_switch_after_gt2')) =} of 2c3, {= pc(C11('4b2', 'shortest_htr_needs_switch_after_gt2')) =} of 4b2, {= pc(C11('2c4', 'shortest_htr_needs_switch_after_gt2')) =} of 2c4, {= pc(C11('2c5', 'shortest_htr_needs_switch_after_gt2')) =} of 2c5. Yura Riabov's line, "More like 3 imo" ([[D11235]]), fits much better: after 4 or more moves it is {= pc(C11('2c3', 'shortest_htr_needs_switch_after_gt3')) =} of 2c3 and {= pc(C11('4b2', 'shortest_htr_needs_switch_after_gt3')) =} of 4b2, large only on 0c4, 4c4 and 2c5. Yura Riabov added "i almost never do this but i think it is useful" and "i really don't like switching after more than 2 moves or for badish cases, which is why i'm worse at finishes than i could be" ([[D11239]], [[D11240]]).
Example: the 2c3 2e `{= WIT('W12', 'scramble') =}` has a single optimal finish, `{= WIT('W12', 'optimal_finish') =}` ({= WIT('W12', 'optimal') =}). Its shortest HTR in any reading is {= WIT('W12', 'shortest_htr_in_any_reading_of_any_optimal') =} moves, and every reading with that HTR switches after {= WIT('W12', 'readings_with_it', 0, 'moves_before_switch') =} moves: `{= ' '.join(WIT('W12', 'optimal_finish').split()[:4]) =}` on normal, then `({= INV(' '.join(WIT('W12', 'optimal_finish').split()[4:])) =})` on inverse.

**e. "I like to check all sub-5 reductions. This can be done super quick and gets optimal like 75% of the time, and it gets optimal +1 the rest of the time"** (Levi Gibson, [[D9108]]; "sub-5" is "4 or less", [[D9221]]).
Writing every reduction to 1q of at most 4 moves, on normal, on inverse and split by NISS, and finishing each reduced state optimally, finds the optimal on {= pc(Q8('2c3', '1', '4', 'found_opt')) =} of 2c3 ({= pc(Q8('2c3 2e', '1', '4', 'found_opt')) =} of 2c3 2e, {= pc(Q8('2c3 4e', '1', '4', 'found_opt')) =} of 2c3 4e) and a finish within +1 on {= pc(Q8('2c3', '1', '4', 'found_p1')) =} (sampled, 4,000 states per subset × edges; {= n(round(float(Q8('2c3', '1', '4', 'mean_reductions_le_R')))) =} reductions on average). On no subset does it reach 75% (the best is 0c0, {= pc(Q8('0c0', '1', '4', 'found_opt')) =}). Levi Gibson used the method mainly on 2c3 and 4a2 ("I don't use it fully for any subsets other than 4a2 and 2c3", [[D9113]]), on 4a2 it is {= pc(Q8('4a2', '1', '4', 'found_opt')) =}, and on 2c3 2e and 4e together with triggers: "For 2c3 2e and 4e I do full DR Bounding + checking the 5-move triggers, and that seems to catch everything" ([[D9117]]); the triggers are not measured here, so this corrects the share for bounding alone. Levi Gibson's later answer to "Would it be around 80 percent?" for 2c3 was a guess ("yeah something like that I'd guess", [[D9209]]), with the advice to check "a bunch of half turns into a 5-move trigger" as well ([[D9212]]).
Example: the 2c3 2e `{= WXX('X1', 'scramble') =}` is {= WXX('X1', 'optimal') =} optimal. All {= WXX('X1', 'reductions_le4') =} different states reached by a reduction to 4a1 4e of at most 4 moves were enumerated on the sticker model and finished optimally from the exact table: the best total is {= WXX('X1', 'best_with_reduction_le4') =}, optimal + 2. The optimal needs a 5-move reduction, `{= WXX('X1', 'normal') =}` on normal, then `{= WXX('X1', 'rest') =}`.

**f. The multi-qt lower bounds of FakeMMAP's reduction bound** ([[T11503]]: "M \geq \frac{|S|(R'+1)-N+\sum_{i edge} \alpha_i}{|S|-1}", with the sum, as in [[T11502]], "over all i's such that i, i+1 are both in S").
Of these lower bounds FakeMMAP wrote "I don't know how useful these bounds really are, I have to put in the numbers to see if there are situations where the typical bound is M \geq 11, rather than M \geq 7 lol." ([[T11504]]); this is that test (the write-up's section 9 gives the typical values). Over {= n(Q9('opt', 'cases')) =} sampled (state, corner path) cases at the optimum, the bounds as written were wrong in {= n(E['t11503_n']) =}: {= n(E['t11503_kinds'].get('first', 0)) =} times the first bound, {= n(E['t11503_kinds'].get('B', 0)) =} times the one with B. Windows i and i+1 share section i+1, so the section counted twice is α_{i+1}, not α_i. With α_{i+1} the bounds held in every case. (The upper bound R of [[T11502]] was never too short.) A second point, for the derivation: C, "the shortest possible HTR in the subset" ([[T11501]]), has to bound HTRs with a switch in the middle, so it must be the shortest NISS HTR, which can be below the linear values FakeMMAP gave ("so 2c3 2e would have C=5"); with the linear C, the exact version of the model is too short on {= pc(Q9('opt', 'insuff_exact_Clin')) =} of cases (the looser formula as written still held).
Example for the index: the 0c4 0e `{= WIT('W13', 'scramble') =}` is {= WIT('W13', 'optimal') =} optimal by `{= WIT('W13', 'optimal_finish') =}`, corner path 0c4 > 4a3 > 4b2 > 4a1 > HTR, N = 4, α = 0, 1, 3, 1, 0. Its sections of half turns are {= ', '.join(map(str, SECTIONS(WIT('W13', 'optimal_finish')))) =}, its 1q reductions {= ', '.join(map(str, WINDOWS(WIT('W13', 'optimal_finish')))) =}, so all reductions shorter than R' = {= min(WINDOWS(WIT('W13', 'optimal_finish'))) =} fail. With S = {0, 2, 3} the bound as written gives M ≥ {= f1(LB11503(9, 4, [0, 2, 3], [0, 1, 3, 1, 0])) =}, so 15, with α_{i+1} {= f1(LB11503(9, 4, [0, 2, 3], [0, 1, 3, 1, 0], True)) =}, so 14, the optimum.
Example for C: the 0c0 2e `{= WIT('W10', 'scramble') =}` is {= WIT('W10', 'optimal') =} optimal by `{= WIT('W10', 'optimal_finish') =}`, read as `{= ' '.join(WIT('W10', 'optimal_finish').split()[:2]) =}` on normal and `({= INV(' '.join(WIT('W10', 'optimal_finish').split()[2:])) =})` on inverse, which is in HTR after {= WIT('W10', 'shortest_htr_in_any_reading_of_any_optimal') =} moves; the shortest linear HTR anywhere in 0c0 2e is {= MINLIN('0c0 2e') =}.
