# Which HTR routes to try from a DR: corrections

*Goes with the write-up on which HTR routes to try from a DR, but reads on its own. The write-up has the measurements and the tables.*

November 2026. A date in brackets is a link to that message on the FMC solvers Discord (#fmc-theory, #fmc-discussion or #non-dr-discussion).

**What everything below is measured against:**

- **The exact optimal finish of every DR state** (all 19,508,428,800, from the first study, proved in Lean there), and the shortest linear HTR of every one of the 29,400 HTR cosets.
- **{= n(25000) =} uniformly random DR states per subset × edges** ({= n(1200000) =} in all), each solved exactly: every optimal and +1 finish, every NISS reading, the shortest HTR on each side and with NISS, every reduction to 1q.

Every scramble and move sequence below was replayed on a sticker-level cube. Each item also says how much of its example is checked in Lean (the study's folder lean/FakeBound: Corrections/Examples.lean and the FakeBound files); the rest is computed, not proved. These are the statements the numbers go against; most of what was said holds up, and that is in the write-up's section 11.

**a. "so yeah, should be 1/6"** (Yura Riabov, [[T4606]], on how much turning the last DR move the other way improves the finish).
Over all DRs the better of X and X·R2 is {= f3(Q5('all', 'mean_gain')) =} moves shorter on average: X·R2 is one move shorter on {= pc(Q5('all', 'share_variation_shorter')) =} of DRs and never more than one, so the gain is about 1/3 of a move, twice the estimate. Yura Riabov called the estimate "very rough" and said "i doubt it will be more than a half move difference with 1 move inverted" ([[T4610]]); that ceiling holds. The first study had the exact third.
Example: think of `{= WIT('W06', 'scramble') =}` as the end of a DR on U/D whose last move was a quarter turn of R (say R); turning that move the other way (R') gives the same state followed by R2. As written the 4b2 2e is {= WIT('W06', 'optimal') =} optimal (`{= WIT('W06', 'optimal_finish') =}`); with R2 appended, `{= WIT('W06', 'variation_scramble') =}`, it is {= WIT('W06', 'variation_optimal') =}: `{= WIT('W06', 'variation_finish') =}`. *Lean-checked*: both optima, both finishes and the subset (`dr_optima_correct`, `dr_solutions_valid`, `labels_htr_routes`). The share and the mean gain are counted over the first study's exact table, not proved in Lean.

**b. "Interesting that it seems 4qt 4C0E is the only HTR subset that is guaranteed 11 moves without NISS"** (Jay, [[T619]]).
Over all 29,400 HTR cosets: 4c4 0e ("4qt 4C0E") has linear HTRs of {= MINLIN('4c4 0e') =} moves, and the one subset whose shortest linear HTR is always {= MINLIN('0c4 8e') =} or more is 0c4 8e (fake HTR corners with 8 bad edges). Lewis Isom noticed the 10 in Aedan Bryant's sheet in 2024 ([[T4104]]: "also under 4qt 4c0e the trigger given is 11 moves but stats say 10 is possible").
Example: `{= WIT('W08', 'scramble') =}` is a 4c4 0e; nissy finds the linear HTR `{= WIT('W08', 'nissy_linear_htr') =}` on the inverse. *Lean-checked*, all of it: the subset, the 10-move linear HTR on the inverse and that the printed one is valid, and that 0c4 8e is the only subset × edges whose shortest linear HTR is never below 11 (`htr_4c4`).

**c. "No, the maximum it takes is 12 moves linearly, and 11 moves using NISS."** (Lewis Isom, [[T4100]], answering whether DR to HTR takes at most 9.)
Both are one short: 0c3 4e and 4c4 0e have HTR cosets whose shortest linear HTR is {= MAXLIN('0c3 4e') =}, and in the sample some DRs need 13 on both sides and 12 with NISS. Daichi had the 13 right later ([[T11594]]: "0c3 4e and 4c4 0e have 13 move optimal HTR cases").
Example: the 0c3 4e `{= WXX('X2', 'scramble') =}` ({= WXX('X2', 'optimal') =} optimal). nissy's shortest linear HTR on either side: `{= WXX('X2', 'nissy_linear') =}`; with NISS: `{= WXX('X2', 'nissy_niss') =}`. *Lean-checked*: the optimum, the subset, the 13-move shortest linear HTR on each side, and that both printed HTRs are valid (`dr_optima_correct`, `htr_13`). That no NISS HTR of 11 exists is from nissy and the study's search, not Lean.

**d. "this isn't the worst practice but it's pretty rare for switching after doing more than 2 moves to actually be useful, unless you're variationmaxing"** (Evan, [[D11196]]).
The optimal through its shortest HTR needs a switch after more than 2 moves on {= pc(C11('all', 'shortest_htr_needs_switch_after_gt2')) =} of DRs (sampled, {= n(25000) =} per subset × edges): take the shortest HTR that any reading of any optimal finish reaches; every reading that reaches it switches after 3 or more moves. That is an upper bound on how often a late switch is needed for the optimal: on some of these DRs another optimal finish, with a longer HTR, may be readable linearly or with an early switch, which is not measured here. Evan named exceptions in the same thread ("the b side of 4c3", "4c4 and 0c4 ofc but those are pretty rare", [[D11237]], [[D11238]]); leaving out all of 4c3, 4c4 and 0c4 it is still {= pc(E['late_excl_gt2']) =}: {= pc(C11('2c3', 'shortest_htr_needs_switch_after_gt2')) =} of 2c3, {= pc(C11('4b2', 'shortest_htr_needs_switch_after_gt2')) =} of 4b2, {= pc(C11('2c4', 'shortest_htr_needs_switch_after_gt2')) =} of 2c4, {= pc(C11('2c5', 'shortest_htr_needs_switch_after_gt2')) =} of 2c5. Yura Riabov's line, "More like 3 imo" ([[D11235]]), fits much better: after 4 or more moves it is {= pc(C11('2c3', 'shortest_htr_needs_switch_after_gt3')) =} of 2c3 and {= pc(C11('4b2', 'shortest_htr_needs_switch_after_gt3')) =} of 4b2, large only on 0c4, 4c4 and 2c5. Yura Riabov added "i almost never do this but i think it is useful" and "i really don't like switching after more than 2 moves or for badish cases, which is why i'm worse at finishes than i could be" ([[D11239]], [[D11240]]).
Example: the 2c3 2e `{= WIT('W12', 'scramble') =}` has a single optimal finish, `{= WIT('W12', 'optimal_finish') =}` ({= WIT('W12', 'optimal') =}). Its shortest HTR in any reading is {= WIT('W12', 'shortest_htr_in_any_reading_of_any_optimal') =} moves, and every reading with that HTR switches after {= WIT('W12', 'readings_with_it', 0, 'moves_before_switch') =} moves: `{= ' '.join(WIT('W12', 'optimal_finish').split()[:4]) =}` on normal, then `({= INV(' '.join(WIT('W12', 'optimal_finish').split()[4:])) =})` on inverse. With a single optimal finish the upper bound is exact: here the late switch is needed. *Lean-checked*: the optimum, the finish and the subset (`dr_optima_correct`, `dr_solutions_valid`, `labels_htr_routes`). That it is the only optimal finish, and the HTR lengths of its readings, come from the study's enumeration (nissy also finds one optimal), not Lean.

On 4a2 the 75% holds: the optimal on {= pc(Q8('4a2', '1', '4', 'found_opt')) =} ({= n(round(float(Q8('4a2', '1', '4', 'mean_reductions_le_R')))) =} reductions on average). **f. FakeMMAP's reduction bound and its multi-qt lower bounds** ([[T11502]]: "R is the max of the following", five families of terms, the sums "over all i's such that i, i+1 are both in S"; [[T11503]]: "M \geq \frac{|S|(R'+1)-N+\sum_{i edge} \alpha_i}{|S|-1}").
Of the lower bounds FakeMMAP wrote "I don't know how useful these bounds really are, I have to put in the numbers to see if there are situations where the typical bound is M \geq 11, rather than M \geq 7 lol." ([[T11504]]); this is that test (the write-up's section 9 gives the typical values). R as written is safe to use: the largest term is never too short, proved for every finish (`Rmax_sound`, lean/FakeBound/FakeBound/Formula.lean), but only because term 1, about M − 1, is among the terms, so it is loose. Two things in the formulas are wrong, each proved in Lean with a counterexample.
*The α index, in both messages.* Windows i and i+1 share section i+1, so the section counted twice is α_{i+1}, not α_i (`cover_sum`, FakeBound/Finish.lean). In the lower bounds: over {= n(E['t11503_counts']['cases']) =} sampled (state, corner path) cases at the optimum, the bounds as written were wrong in {= n(E['t11503_n']) =}: {= n(E['t11503_kinds'].get('first', 0)) =} times the first bound, {= n(E['t11503_kinds'].get('B', 0)) =} times the one with B. With α_{i+1} they were never wrong ({= n(E['t11503_counts']['violations_alpha_i_plus_1']) =} cases), and they are proved valid for every finish (`lower_free`, `lower_A`, `lower_B`, `lower_C`, FakeBound/Finish.lean). In the terms of the upper bound the same index makes a term too short (`alpha_i_fails`, FakeBound/Counter.lean): on the path 4b2 > 4a3 > 4b2 > 4a1 > 0c0, a 14-move finish with sections 2, 1, 3, 1, 3 needs a 9-move reduction, but the smallest term with α_i is 8. Under "max" that does no harm; under the smallest-term reading below it does.
Example for the index: the 0c4 0e `{= WIT('W13', 'scramble') =}` is {= WIT('W13', 'optimal') =} optimal by `{= WIT('W13', 'optimal_finish') =}`, corner path 0c4 > 4a3 > 4b2 > 4a1 > HTR, N = 4, α = 0, 1, 3, 1, 0. Its sections of half turns are {= ', '.join(map(str, SECTIONS(WIT('W13', 'optimal_finish')))) =}, its 1q reductions {= ', '.join(map(str, WINDOWS(WIT('W13', 'optimal_finish')))) =}, so all reductions shorter than R' = {= min(WINDOWS(WIT('W13', 'optimal_finish'))) =} fail. With S = {0, 2, 3} the bound as written gives M ≥ {= f1(LB11503(9, 4, [0, 2, 3], [0, 1, 3, 1, 0])) =}, so 15, with α_{i+1} {= f1(LB11503(9, 4, [0, 2, 3], [0, 1, 3, 1, 0], True)) =}, so 14, the optimum. *Lean-checked end to end*: the optimum, the finish, the subset, the corner path, α, the sections, the reductions and both inequalities (`dr_optima_correct`, `dr_solutions_valid`, `labels_htr_routes`, `index_example`, `index_example_arith`, Corrections/Examples.lean).
*Term 2's signs, when R is the smallest term.* Every term is an upper bound on the reduction a finish needs, so the bound the derivation gives is the smallest term, and with α_{i+1} and term 2 as derived it is sound for every finish (`Rmin_sound`, FakeBound/Formula.lean; it is not always the exact worst case, `Rmin_not_tight`). Term 2 as printed, "\frac{(|S|-1)M-(N-|S|)+\sum_{i edge} \alpha_i}{|S|}", has both signs flipped: the derivation gives ((|S|−1)M + (N−|S|) − Σα)/|S|, and so does FakeMMAP's own first lower bound ([[T11503]]) solved for R'. With the printed term 2 the smallest term is too short: on a 10-move finish with sections 0, 3, 0, 4 (path 4a1 > 0c0 > 4a1 > 0c0) the reductions are 6, 6, 5, but printed term 2 with S = {0, 2} gives 4, whatever A, B and C are, and still 4 with the printed α_i as well (`printed_term2_fails`, `literal_min_fails`, FakeBound/Counter.lean). In the sample it is too short on {= pc(Q9('opt', 'insuff_min_terms_as_written')) =} of cases at the optimum.
Example for the signs: the 0c3 2e `{= WIT('W14', 'scramble') =}` is {= WIT('W14', 'optimal') =} optimal by `{= WIT('W14', 'optimal_finish') =}`, and every optimal finish takes the path 0c3 > 4a2 > 4a1 > HTR (N = 3, α = 0, 3, 3, 0). Its sections are {= ', '.join(map(str, SECTIONS(WIT('W14', 'optimal_finish')))) =}, so its 1q reductions are {= ', '.join(map(str, WINDOWS(WIT('W14', 'optimal_finish')))) =} and a 7-move reduction is needed. Term 2 as printed with S = {0, 2} gives {= f1(T2(14, 3, [0, 2], [0, 3, 3, 0])) =}, so 6; derived, {= f1(T2(14, 3, [0, 2], [0, 3, 3, 0], True)) =}, so 7. *Lean-checked*: the arithmetic from the sections on (`witness_0c3`). The state's optimum and its paths come from the exact table and the study's enumeration, not Lean.
*An earlier version of this entry (until 12 Nov 2026) also said that C, "the shortest possible HTR in the subset", must be the shortest NISS HTR. That was wrong: C bounds the middle of the finish, a linear HTR of a DR in the same subset (half turns keep the subset, proved in Lean: `labels_keep`), so FakeMMAP's linear C is right (section 9 of the write-up). The mistake was to bound the finish minus one middle section instead, which is a NISS HTR of the DR itself: the 0c0 2e `F2 U' B2 R2 B2 U R2` is 7 optimal by `R2 U' B2 R2 B2 U F2`, and without its middle `B2 R2 B2` it leaves `R2 U'` on normal and `(F2 U')` on inverse, a 4-move NISS HTR, below the subset's shortest linear HTR of 5 (Lean-checked: `C_example`, `dr_optima_correct`).*
