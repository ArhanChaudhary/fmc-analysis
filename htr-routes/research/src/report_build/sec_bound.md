## 8. DR bounding

The method of Levi Gibson's *DR Bounding* doc: "1. Find many reductions to the 1qt 4c4e subset … 2. For each case, create a lower bound of how good the solution can be … 3. Start checking the 1qt cases from best to worst … 4. Once you find a solution, cross off all the 1QT cases that can not be solved in less moves than the solution you found." rodneykinney's *Advanced DR Bounding* sharpens the bounds and adds 2qt. Here I measure the method's ceiling: if you write every reduction to 1q of at most R moves (on normal, on inverse, or split between them, section 3) and finish each reduced state optimally, with however many quarter turns that takes, how often is one of them the optimal finish of the DR, and how many reductions is that? The search over reductions is exact; reductions are counted as a solver writes them (each side's sequence empty or ending in a quarter turn), exactly by table. Sampled: the first 4,000 states per subset × edges; shares have standard errors of at most {= f1(E['q8_se_max_corner'] * 100) =} points per corner subset.

{{bounding}}

Each cell: share of DRs where some reduction of at most R moves leads to an optimal finish / mean number of reductions of at most R moves. Counts for R = 7 are not reported: the table that counts them was checked against brute force only up to 5 moves on every subset and up to 7 on 40 states of 0c0, so for R ≤ 7 the table gives the share only (that comes from the exact search). The last three columns: a finish within +1 with reductions of at most 4; a finish of 12 or better with reductions of at most 5 (for DRs that have one); reductions to 2q of at most 3 moves.

What it says:

- **Which subsets suit it depends on R.** With reductions of at most 4, bounding finds the optimal on {= pc(Q8('0c0', '1', '4', 'found_opt')) =} of 0c0, {= pc(Q8('4a2', '1', '4', 'found_opt')) =} of 4a2, {= pc(Q8('4a1', '1', '4', 'found_opt')) =} of 4a1, {= pc(Q8('4b2', '1', '4', 'found_opt')) =} of 4b2 and {= pc(Q8('2c3', '1', '4', 'found_opt')) =} of 2c3; at most 6 puts 4a1 ({= pc(Q8('4a1', '1', '6', 'found_opt')) =}) and 4b2 ({= pc(Q8('4b2', '1', '6', 'found_opt')) =}) ahead of 4a2 ({= pc(Q8('4a2', '1', '6', 'found_opt')) =}). On 2c5 and 4b5 no reduction of 5 or less reaches 4a1 4e at all (it takes at least 6), and 4c4 and 2c4 are close to that.
- **Each extra move of reduction costs a lot**: the number of reductions grows {= f1(E['q8_growth'][0]) =} to {= f1(E['q8_growth'][1]) =} times from 5 to 6 moves. Over all DRs it is {= n(round(float(Q8('all', '1', '4', 'mean_reductions_le_R')))) =} reductions on average at most 4, {= n(round(float(Q8('all', '1', '5', 'mean_reductions_le_R')))) =} at most 5, {= n(round(float(Q8('all', '1', '6', 'mean_reductions_le_R')))) =} at most 6. With the bounds doing their job you finish only a few of them, but you still write them, and NISS-trace them.
- **2q needs much shorter reductions**: at most 3 moves to a 2q state reaches the optimal on {= pc(Q8('all', '2', '3', 'found_opt')) =} of all DRs, against {= pc(Q8('all', '1', '3', 'found_opt')) =} for 1q. But each 2q state still needs its own finish found by hand, which is the hard part bounding at 1q avoids.
- **Within +1** is much easier: at most 4 moves gives a finish within +1 on {= pc(Q8('all', '1', '4', 'found_p1')) =} of DRs.
- **The one-quarter-turn reading** (the reduction, then a finish with exactly one quarter turn, which is what JTuu's simulation counted) finds less: on 2c3 {= pc(E['q8w']['2c3|1|4']['found_opt']) =} at R ≤ 4 against {= pc(Q8('2c3', '1', '4', 'found_opt')) =}, on 4b2 {= pc(E['q8w']['4b2|1|4']['found_opt']) =} against {= pc(Q8('4b2', '1', '4', 'found_opt')) =}.

**Against the checklists.** For the question I was asked (how often bounding finds the optimal per subset, and how many reductions that takes): the smallest R for which bounding finds the optimal on 80% of a subset, with the reductions that is, against how many routes the checklist of section 4 needs for 80%:

{{bounding_vs_checklist}}

Bounding gets to 80% with short reductions on 0c0 (3), 4a2 and 4a1 (5), where the budget-2 checklist struggles (0c0 and 4a1 stop short of 80% after eight routes); on 4b2 it needs every reduction up to 6, about {= n(round(float(Q8('4b2', '1', '6', 'mean_reductions_le_R')))) =} of them, where the checklist stops at {= BUD('4b2', '2', 8) =}. On 2c4, 2c5, 4b5, 0c3, 0c4, 4c3 and 4c4 one to five routes reach 80%, and bounding needs 6 or 7 moves of reduction or does not get there by 7. A route and a reduction are not the same amount of work, so the comparison is only in kind. **For a human the bounding column is mostly out of reach**: each extra move of reduction multiplies the reductions by about eight, and a solver in an attempt writes tens, perhaps a couple of hundred (R ≤ 4 is about {= n(round(float(Q8('all', '1', '4', 'mean_reductions_le_R')))) =} on average). So only the 0c0 entry (R = 3, about {= n(round(float(Q8('0c0', '1', '3', 'mean_reductions_le_R')))) =} reductions) is a practical recommendation; every entry with R of 5 or more, thousands of reductions, is **computer only**, there to show where the optimal lies, not how to find it. The R ≤ 4 column of section 8 is the one to plan an attempt with.

**Levi Gibson's verdicts per subset.** After "lots and lots of solves" ([[T7074]]): "0c0: Yes", "4a2: yes yes yes amazing muah chief's kiss", for 4a1 "if you get a 4a1 case randomly, bound it. Otherwise it's not great. For the 2e/6e case it's not great because you 99% of the time you want to find a 0c0 or 4b2 trigger", for 4b2 "just check the really short ones, and then just try triggers for the rest", for 2c3 "I only use it for the 6e subset now. Triggers will get you optimal usually for 2e and 4e", and "Bounding is actually REALLY good for 4c3". Of the subsets Levi Gibson named as the ones for full bounding ("I don't use it fully for any subsets other than 4a2 and 2c3", [[D9113]]), 4a2 gets {= pc(Q8('4a2', '1', '4', 'found_opt')) =}. So for 2c3 both halves are off for bounding alone: the optimal comes from a reduction of 4 or less on under half of 2c3, and on {= pc(1 - float(Q8('2c3', '1', '4', 'found_p1'))) =} not even +1 does.
