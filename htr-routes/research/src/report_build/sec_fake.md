## 9. FakeMMAP's reduction-length bound

**What was proposed.** FakeMMAP ([[T11501]]): "Let's say we're looking for a finish in M moves (or less), assuming we're only allowed to use N>1 qts. We want to know how long our reductions to 1q should be, in order to find that solution. Let R be the biggest possible length of a reduction to 1q you have to check". The inputs are A and B, the optimal HTR on normal and on inverse, C, "the shortest possible HTR in the subset" ("so 2c3 2e would have C=5, 4b2 0e would have C=6"), and section bounds: "Numbering these sections from 0 to N, let \alpha_i be the bound for the ith section. For example 4a3 2e would have \alpha_1=3, because you have to to the 4b2 parity fix." Then ([[T11502]]) "R is the max of the following", five families of terms over sets S of consecutive windows, solved "with Fourier-Motzkin elimination by hand"; and ([[T11503]]) "multi-qt DR bounding... well, sort of": lower bounds on M once every reduction shorter than R' has failed. Of the lower bounds FakeMMAP added "I don't know how useful these bounds really are, I have to put in the numbers to see if there are situations where the typical bound is M \geq 11, rather than M \geq 7 lol." ([[T11504]]). This section puts in the numbers, for the upper bound R as well.

**How I tested it.** A finish with N quarter turns has N + 1 sections of half turns, s_0 … s_N, each at least α_i; the 1q reduction that keeps quarter turn q with its two neighbouring sections has length (finish length) − 1 − s_{q−1} − s_q. I computed α_i for every corner path that occurs (fewest half turns section i needs over every corner arrangement following the path; for 4a3 > 4b2 > 4a1 > HTR it gives {= E['alpha_4a3'] =}, FakeMMAP's α_1 = 3; this study's Lean certificate re-derives every α (appendix B)). Then, for every sampled state, every corner path of its optimal (or +1, or ≤ 10, 11, 12) finishes, and the shortest reduction such a finish needs, I compared that need with R three ways: the formula of [[T11502]] as written; the same without its first term; and the exact optimum of the same linear model (the largest shortest-reduction any section lengths allowed by the constraints can force), solved by enumeration instead of by hand.

**(a) It is sufficient.** As written, R was never shorter than what a finish needed, in any case, at any target. The derivation does need C to bound every HTR you can reach with a switch in the middle, which makes C the shortest NISS HTR, not the shortest linear one. FakeMMAP's examples (C = 5 for 2c3 2e) are linear minimums, and NISS HTRs go below them: on 2c3 2e as low as 4 (tseitsei89: "NISS HTR can be as low as 4 moves", [[D14620]]). With the linear C the exact model is too short on {= pc(Q9('opt', 'insuff_exact_Clin')) =} of cases at the optimum ({= pc(Q9('M10', 'insuff_exact_Clin')) =} when hunting a 10). Example: the 0c0 2e of `{= WIT('W10', 'scramble') =}` is {= WIT('W10', 'optimal') =} optimal through a NISS HTR of {= WIT('W10', 'shortest_htr_in_any_reading_of_any_optimal') =} moves, below the subset's linear minimum of {= MINLIN('0c0 2e') =} (replayed). The formula as written survives this because it is loose; with C the shortest NISS HTR the exact model is never too short either.

**(b) It does not prune at the reduction lengths solvers write.** At the optimum, with the corner path known, R averages {= f1(Q9('opt', 'mean_R_literal')) =} as written, {= f1(Q9('opt', 'mean_R_no_term1')) =} without term 1 and {= f1(Q9('opt', 'mean_R_exact_Cniss')) =} in the exact model, where the finishes needed {= f1(Q9('opt', 'mean_R_needed')) =} on average and {= Q9('opt', 'max') =} at most. Not knowing which corner path the optimal takes (worst case over the subset's paths, with the DR's own A and B), R averages {= f1(Q9('opt', 'mean_Rstate_literal')) =} as written, {= f1(Q9('opt', 'mean_Rstate_no_term1')) =} without term 1 and {= f1(Q9('opt', 'mean_Rstate_exact_Cniss')) =} exactly, against a longest need per subset × edges of {= f1(Q9('opt', 'R_empirical_max')) =} on average: the formula lets you skip nothing compared with checking every reduction up to that longest need, the exact model next to nothing. Hunting a fixed M is the same story: for 12 or better, path unknown, R is {= f1(Q9('M12', 'mean_Rstate_literal')) =} as written, {= f1(Q9('M12', 'mean_Rstate_no_term1')) =} without term 1 and {= f1(Q9('M12', 'mean_Rstate_exact_Cniss')) =} exactly; the longest need per subset × edges averages {= f1(Q9('M12', 'R_empirical_max')) =} and is {= Q9('M12', 'max') =} at most. Solvers check reductions of 4 to 6 moves (section 8), and a bound that says *go to 9 or 10* never tells you to stop earlier than that. Where the bound could help is the other direction, proving that a short M is impossible, and that is (c).

**(c) The multi-qt lower bounds, and one index.** The first bound of [[T11503]] is M ≥ (|S|(R'+1) − N + Σ α_i) / (|S| − 1), the sum "over all i's such that i, i+1 are both in S" (as in [[T11502]]). Windows i and i+1 share section i+1, so the section that is counted twice is α_{i+1}, not α_i. As written, the bounds are wrong in {= n(E['t11503_n']) =} of {= n(E['t11503_cases_opt']) =} cases at the optimum (subsets {= ', '.join(E['t11503_subsets']) =}): {= n(E['t11503_kinds'].get('first', 0)) =} times this first bound and {= n(E['t11503_kinds'].get('B', 0)) =} times the one with B. With α_{i+1} they hold in every case, and R stays sufficient. Example, replayed: the 0c4 0e of `{= WIT('W13', 'scramble') =}` is {= WIT('W13', 'optimal') =} optimal by `{= WIT('W13', 'optimal_finish') =}`, path 0c4 > 4a3 > 4b2 > 4a1 > HTR (N = 4), α = 0, 1, 3, 1, 0. Its sections are {= ', '.join(map(str, SECTIONS(WIT('W13', 'optimal_finish')))) =} and its 1q reductions {= ', '.join(map(str, WINDOWS(WIT('W13', 'optimal_finish')))) =} moves, so every reduction shorter than R' = {= min(WINDOWS(WIT('W13', 'optimal_finish'))) =} fails. With S = {0, 2, 3} the bound as written adds α_2 = 3 and gives M ≥ {= f1(LB11503(9, 4, [0, 2, 3], [0, 1, 3, 1, 0])) =}, so 15, but the finish is 14; with α_3 = 1 it gives {= f1(LB11503(9, 4, [0, 2, 3], [0, 1, 3, 1, 0], True)) =}, so 14. I take the shared section to be what was meant.
