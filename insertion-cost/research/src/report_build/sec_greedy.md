Nobody searches every pair of comms by hand. You find the best first comm, perhaps note a few more, and then look for the second in each. Insertion Finder's *greedy* setting is the same idea, in its own words: "Let's say when searching insertions for each step, the minimum moves of all skeletons is M. IF will keep all skeletons whose moves equal or less than M + N moves. 0 means always best first (human mode?)." So two questions: how good is the best first comm, and what does following only the best ones lose?

An 8-3 or better is there on {= CELL('greedy_first', '20', '8-3 or better') =} of 20-move 5c skeletons and {= CELL('greedy_first', '16', '8-3 or better') =} of 16-move ones; an 8-4 or better on {= CELL('greedy_first', '20', '8-4 or better') =} and {= CELL('greedy_first', '16', '8-4 or better') =}. So an 8-3 most of the time, an 8-4 about one time in {= GONEIN('16', '8-4 or better') =} on a 16-move skeleton.

**Following only the best first comms: two 8-move comms on random skeletons of 20 moves. *Best first comm only* is the best first comm (all that tie for it) followed by the best second; *within 1 move* also follows the first comms one move worse. *Loses* is the share of skeletons where the result is longer than the best pair. A rule that ends with no second comm after the first comms it follows counts as a loss and is left out of that rule's mean; for the best first comm only, the sixth column says on how many skeletons that happens.**

{{greedy}}

- **Taking the best first comm of a 5c and then the best second gives {= CELL('greedy', '5c', 'Best first comm only') =} against {= CELL('greedy', '5c', 'Best pair of comms') =} for the best pair**, and misses the best pair on {= GLOSS('5c', 0) =} of skeletons. (All first comms that tie for the best are followed up.)
- **Also following the first comms that are one move worse** brings that to {= GLOSS('5c', 1) =}, and two moves worse to {= GLOSS('5c', 2) =}.
- This is what kyh ([[N5837]], [[N5838]]) and argonblue ([[N5842]]) ran into: "my pr single wouldve been a 30 if i didnt go look for sub optimal first insertions", "but its a 28"; "i just had to check a suboptimal first insertion (8-2 instead of 8-3) to get any cancellations in the second". The second comm depends on where the first one went, so the best first comm is not always the one to follow.
- **What people say about the setting.** Ben ([[N5683]], [[N5684]]): "if you set greedy to 0 and only pick regular 3c comms then it basically shows human findable", "the main thing is setting it to greedy 0". And ([[N5688]], [[N5689]]): "greedy 1 is still reasonable if your skeleton is really good but anything higher isn't imo", "like if i have an 8-3 im not checking 8-1s ever". kyh ([[N5836]]): "i think 1 is realistic". What is findable is not measured here. What each setting gives on a 20-move 5c with 8-movers is: {= CELL('greedy', '5c', 'Best first comm only') =} at greedy 0, {= CELL('greedy', '5c', 'First comms within 1 move') =} at greedy 1, {= CELL('greedy', '5c', 'Best pair of comms') =} for the best pair. By hand, greedy 1 means: when the best first comm is an 8-4, also follow up the 8-3s.
- **The other two-comm leftovers** are in the same table, with the same pattern: one move of slack on the first comm recovers most of the loss. A twisted 2c2c still loses on {= CELL('greedy', 'twisted 2c2c', 'Within 1 move: loses') =} of skeletons with it and a 3c3c on {= CELL('greedy', '3c3c', 'Within 1 move: loses') =}.
