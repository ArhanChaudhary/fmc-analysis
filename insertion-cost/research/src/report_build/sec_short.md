**What is new here**

- **Which 2c2c is the cheap one depends on the alg list.** With 8-movers a twisted 2c2c costs {= M('2c2c-twisted') =} and a pure one {= M('2c2c-pure') =}. With every alg up to 10 moves it flips: pure {= M('2c2c-pure', L=10) =}, twisted {= M('2c2c-twisted', L=10) =} ({= NSK('2c2c-pure', L=10) =} skeletons each), because only the pure one has 10-move algs; a twisted one has none under 12. That is cpaul's order ("9 for pure 2-2", "9.5 for 2-2 with a twist").
- **By cost a 2c2c beats a 3c1t, and a 3c1t beats a 5c**, which answers jisu101's "2c2c, 3c1t, or 5c?" ([[N6504]], [[N6505]]). A 2c2c is {= DIFF('3c1t', '2c2c-pure') =} to {= DIFF('3c1t', '2c2c-twisted') =} cheaper than a 3c1t (20 moves, 8-movers), so argonblue's "3c1t > 2c2c > 5c" has the first two the other way round (a typical skeleton of each: example [a] in section 10). It is about a move on average, and the spread from one skeleton to the next is wider than that, so it is hard to notice solve by solve.
- **wen's question, answered** ("Why is there even a difference if the number of possible first insertions is the same tbh", [[N4862]]). For the two kinds of 2c2c the number is the same, {= FC('2c2c-pure') =} first comms per spot, and when the two comms do not touch they cost the same ({= CELL('nesting', 'pure 2c2c', 'The two not touching') =} and {= CELL('nesting', 'twisted 2c2c', 'The two not touching') =}). The {= GAP2C2C('Two comms, nesting allowed') =} between them comes from comms that interact, {= GAP2C2CNEST() =} of it from nesting: the best twisted pair is nested on {= CELL('nesting', 'twisted 2c2c', 'Best needs nesting') =} of skeletons, the best pure pair on {= CELL('nesting', 'pure 2c2c', 'Best needs nesting') =}. Across types the number is not the same: {= FC('2tc') =} for a 2tc, {= FC('2c2c-pure') =} for a 2c2c, {= FC('3tc') =} for a 3tc, {= FC('3c1t') =} for a 3c1t, {= FC('5c') =} for a 5c, and the costs come in that order (first line of the next block).
- **A longer alg list is worth far more on two comms than on one.** On the same skeletons under every cap ({= PAIRN('5c') =} of 20 moves per type), a pure 2c2c goes from {= PAIR('2c2c-pure', '8') =} to {= PAIR('2c2c-pure', '10') =} to {= PAIR('2c2c-pure', '12') =} at caps 8, 10 and 12, and a 5c from {= PAIR('5c', '8') =} to {= PAIR('5c', '10') =} to {= PAIR('5c', '12') =}. A 3tc goes from {= PAIR('3tc', '10') =} at cap 10 to {= PAIR('3tc', '12') =} at cap 12, all of it from longer comms, since no 3tc alg is 12 moves or fewer. Where a 10-move pure 2c2c alg fits ({= CELL('direct', 'pure 2c2c', 'Alg of 10 or less: fits somewhere') =} of skeletons) it beats two 8-movers by {= f2(float(CELL('direct', 'pure 2c2c', 'Two 8-move comms on the skeletons where an alg of 10 or less fits')) - float(CELL('direct', 'pure 2c2c', 'Alg of 10 or less: mean added'))) =}.
- **Longer algs barely help a 3c**: at 20 moves, {= M('3c', L=10) =} with every alg up to 10 moves and {= M('3c', L=12) =} up to 12, against {= M('3c') =}.
